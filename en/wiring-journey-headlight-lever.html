The column lever:what's inside

This is about the lower column lever, the one sticking out from under the steering wheel. In Part 8 we dismissed flashing in a few words as “one exception”; here we open it up all the way.

What this supplement stands on. Everything here is read from the 500D's factory documents — the owner's handbook and the “controls and instruments” chapter of the workshop manual. ⚠️ The F, L and R column switches are different parts (different numbers in parts dealers' fitment tables, and a different number of wires). They work the same way, but we can't say they're built the same inside. Please read this supplement as “this is how it is on the D”.

Wires coming out of the column lever:there are seven

Follow this column lever on the wiring diagram and seven wires come out of the bundle. Four are inputs and three are outputs. Having as many as four inputs is the most distinctive thing about this part.

TerminalInsulation colourDirectionComes from / goes to
Cwhite (BIANCO)InFrom the light switch on the dash. The workshop manual says “this switch is fed from the main switch on the instrument panel”
INTblack (NERO)InFrom the exterior lighting feed (30/4 on the ignition switch). ⭐ It doesn't pass through the light switch
30/2pink (ROSA)InFrom after fuse F6
30/3brown (MARRONE)InFrom after fuse F5
56bgrey (GRIGIO)OutTo the low beams (split into fuses F3 and F4 further on)
56/a2green (VERDE)OutTo the right high beam
56/a1green + black (VERDE E NERO)OutTo the left high beam (the high-beam indicator lamp is here too)

Terminal names are exactly as written on the D's factory wiring diagram. Colour names are the original's Italian, with an English gloss.

The same seven, laid out in their wire colours. ⭐ Four inputs is what sets this part apart; of them, only C comes after the light switch on the dash, and INT doesn't pass through it. F5 and F6 on the left are fuses. What hangs off beyond them isn't followed in this diagram.

These seven alone explain the “crossed” pattern from Part 8. ⭐ The high-beam current comes in from after the side-light fuses (F5, F6). 30/3 (from F5) goes to the left high beam, 30/2 (from F6) to the right one. So when one fuse blows, “the high beam on one side” and “the side lights on the opposite side” go out together — the combination we saw in Part 8 is made right here.

The low beams are different, though. Their current comes in at C — that is, straight from the light switch on the dash — passes through the column lever and leaves at 56b. The fuses come after that (F3, F4).

Position by position,what connects inside

The further down you push the column lever, the further it steps. Laying what the owner's handbook says is “lit in that position” over the terminal table above gives this.

Column leverWhat lights (handbook)Connected inside
INumber plate light, side lights, tail lightsNothing goes out to the headlights
II+ low beamsC → 56b
III+ high beams30/3 → 56/a1 (left) and 30/2 → 56/a2 (right)
Pull towards youLow beams light (flashing)INT → 56b

⚠️ “Connected inside” is a reading put together from the terminal names and destinations. The original wiring diagram doesn't draw the inside of the column lever — it only shows where the seven come from and go to.

Flashing alonecomes in by a different entrance

Look at the table again. The low beams in II come from C, the low beams when flashing come from INT — the same bulbs are lit through two different entrances.

That's why you can flash even with the light switch off. C is after the light switch, but INT doesn't pass through it. In a German 500 owners' community, someone who replaced their column switch checked with a meter and reported “when flashing, the two that conduct are 56b and INT”, which matches this reading.

Useful for narrowing it down The low beams don't come on, but flashing works. — then the bulbs, the ground and the 56b wire are all alive (flashing uses that same path). Suspect the side from the light switch to C.
Conversely, if only flashing doesn't work, it's the INT wire or the flashing mechanism inside the column lever — the push pin in the next section.

⚠️ The 500D owner's handbook says you can pull it towards you “in every position except II”. Reading the workshop manual, at position II the drum has no contact face, so it can't press the push pin — it's excluded by the mechanism, not because the low beams are already on and you can't see a change. ⚠️ However, the F and L handbooks have no such proviso, and it conflicts with what owners of real cars report. Check on your own car.

What's inside

The workshop manual describes the inside of this part in a single paragraph. ⭐ A rotating drum and fixed contacts embedded in a resin plate — and for flashing alone, an entirely separate mechanism.

1 Rotating drum. Move the column lever and this cylinder turns.
2 Spring-loaded contact bridge. It rides on the drum and, where it turns to, bridges the fixed contacts.
3 Fixed contacts. Embedded in an insulating resin plate; from here they connect to the outside wires.
4 ⭐A separate mechanism just for flashing. The cam face on the side of the drum presses the push pin, which closes the elastic contact beneath it. It isn't a mechanism that selects a step but one that is closed only while pressed.

⚠️ This diagram explains the construction. The workshop manual describes it in words but doesn't say which contact is which terminal. So the contacts in the diagram carry no terminal names (the INT and 56b at the bottom just mark the two wires the flashing mechanism joins).

How it fails:the original lists five

The workshop manual's “exterior lighting switch” section lists five symptoms and causes. ⚠️ And all five end with the same sentence — “replace the switch assembly”. ⭐ The original treats this part from the start as “something you replace, not repair”.

SymptomCauses given in the original
When flashing, the contact connects only intermittentlyToo much play between drum and seat / worn contact bridge or fixed contacts / stuck contact bridge / broken or weakened pressure spring
⭐ The low beams stay on whatever position the column lever is inPush pin doesn't return / weakened elastic contact
Flashing doesn't light anythingBroken moving contact / oxidised contacts / worn push pin / worn cam face on the drum / loose wiring on the fixed-contact side
The column lever's detent (the “click” you feel) is weak or missingThe spring-loaded ball sticks too far out of its seat
The column lever is stiff or won't moveSame as above

The second is a telling example. The low beams stay on whatever position the column lever is in — it looks like a short somewhere in the wiring, but the original says to suspect first a push pin that hasn't returned. ⭐ The flashing mechanism is stuck “pressed”. INT doesn't pass through the light switch, so in this state they shouldn't go out even if you switch the light switch off.

What's outside this supplement
① The inside of the F, L and R column switches = they're different parts, so the construction described here can't simply be applied to them.
② The indicator lever = the upper lever is a separate switch, also different inside.
③ Which contact is which terminal = nowhere in the original does it say which fixed contact is which terminal.
④ Where it is on the real car = the parts of this circuit haven't been measured yet, so there's no location diagram.
