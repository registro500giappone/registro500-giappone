The horn doesn't sound

You press the button and get nothing at all. — The horn circuit is the opposite in two ways of the routes we've followed so far. One: it's live without turning the key. Two: the switch sits on the way-back side.

This story is about the FIAT 500 (D, F, L and R). The wiring is taken from the 500's original sources.

The button is pressed. The key position doesn't matter — on a 500 the horn should sound even with the key out. Yet there's no sound: that's the symptom on this page.

The horn's purple wire stays live even with the key out. It's after fuse F1 = a circuit fed from the battery all the time. Before touching the wiring, disconnect the battery's negative (−) terminal. Let a tool touch the body and it short-circuits straight away.
⚠️ If the horn gets stuck on, disconnecting the battery's negative terminal also comes first (if a wire touches ground in the button or the column, it keeps sounding without being pressed). A horn sounding continuously gets hot.
⚠️ The 500's horn circuit has no relay (there is none in the original wiring diagram). The horn current goes straight through the thin button contact inside the steering wheel — which is why it wears.
How it works

The button doesn't “send” current;it connects the way back

  1. Battery current first goes to regulator terminal 30 at the back of the car, then comes back to the fuse box at the front (the same route as Parts 1–4).
  2. Through fuse F1. ⚠️ In Part 4 this fuse was drawn as a branch hanging off to the side, because the red wire to the key branches off before the fuse. The horn is after the fuse — so this time the main line runs vertically through it.
  3. Leaving the fuse, the purple wire (VIOLA) brings 12V to the horn's positive (+) terminal. Up to here it's always live, regardless of the key.
  4. From the horn's negative terminal, the yellow/black wire runs through the steering column to the horn button. Press the button and it drops to the body (ground) there, closing the loop.

Wire colours are the real insulation colours (VIOLA = purple, GIALLO E NERO = yellow/black, NERO = black). Yellow dots = where current is flowing now. The round tags on the wires are terminal names. The dark tags are the two places we'll put the meter on = the horn's + and −. The winding inside the horn is drawn as a thin light line — it's the inside of a part, not a wire in the original. Only the terminal after the fuse says “(no name)” — because it has no name in the original wiring diagram, and on the real car you won't find a number there either.

Why is the switch on the “way back” side? The steering wheel turns. Running a ground wire through something that turns is safer than running a 12V wire (if it chafes and touches the body, the horn just sounds). For the same reason, the oil pressure warning light (Part 2) is also grounded at the sender.
Narrowing it down

Just measure the horn's two terminalsand the search splits in two

There are two places to measure: the horn's positive terminal and its negative terminal. Put the meter's black lead on the battery's negative terminal. The key can stay OFF.

Measure in this order and you can tell “wire or part” without removing the horn. The most common pattern is 12V at ② that doesn't drop at ③ (inside the column and the button contacts are where things move most and wear out).

When something is wrong ①

Fuse blown =F1, which has nothing to do with the key,is the culprit here

F1 blown. Everything below the fuse has faded. In Parts 1, 4 and 6 we kept saying “checking the fuse won't fix it”, but that was about circuits that branch off before the fuse. The horn is after the fuse. And the fuse that blows is not the key-ON F2 that was the culprit in Parts 7, 10 and 11, but F1, which is live regardless of the key.

⭐ Only two things are after F1: the horn and the interior light (the original fuse table). So “horn silent + interior light off too” is almost always explained by one fuse. Conversely, the charge lamp, the starter and the ignition all live independently of F1 — in this diagram you can see that from the wire to the key branching off before the fuse.

When something is wrong ②

12V on both terminals, yetsilent

The button ground has come off. Both the horn's positive and negative have 12V, and the wires keep their colour. Even so no current flows — there's no way back.

This is “stays at 12V” at ③ in the table above. Voltage wherever you measure, yet nothing happens — this shape also appeared in Part 6 (ignition primary). Electricity depends not only on “does it arrive” but on “can it get back”: the backbone shared by all the parts so far (→ Part 5).

⭐ The factory workshop manual lists six causes of “horn doesn't sound” — ① blown fuse ② broken or loose wiring ③ ⭐ dirt or wear on the ring-shaped contact at the centre of the steering wheel ④ faulty button contact ⑤ bad ground on the horn itself ⑥ worn contacts or misadjustment inside the horn.
⭐ ③ is a place this diagram can't draw. The button turns with the wheel, so a ring contact that keeps touching as it turns is used to pass current to it. It wears the most, yet it's the hardest to see — if you get stuck at ③ in the table above, this is the next thing to open.

Where it is on the car

The horn is at the front,the button in the column

Both the ● fuse box and the ● horn are at the front of the car. The horn sits behind the front panel (inside the grille), the spot that takes the most rain and mud. Corroded terminals and a rusty ground are the most common causes of this symptom.

⚠️ ● The horn button mark points to the centre of the steering wheel. The button itself is at the end of a wire that comes down through the column, so its position can't be shown as a single point on a map seen from above.

Once fixed

Fuse replaced.Press the buttonand it sounds again.

The key is still OFF; we just pressed the button. Current comes down the purple wire, through the horn's winding, up the column and to ground at the button — the moment this loop closes, it sounds.

⚠️ If the new fuse blows again, stop replacing it and look for the cause. It blows because the purple or yellow/black wire is touching ground somewhere, and the fuse is taking the hit in its place. Fit a bigger fuse and the next thing to burn is the wire.
