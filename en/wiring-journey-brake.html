The brake lights don't come on

A lamp you can't see yourself (the wiring diagram calls it the stop lamp; it's the same thing). Usually someone else notices, or the inspection station. — The circuit is short, yet it has two symptoms: "won't light" and "stays on". And because there are two lamps, with the current crossing from one to the other, which one is out tells you where the culprit is.

This story is about the FIAT 500 (D, F, L and R models). The wiring is drawn from the original 500 sources.

You press the brake pedal, yet the two lenses at the back stay dark — that's the symptom on this page. First check the key position: on a 500, with the key OFF the brake lights don't come on (that's normal; the reason is explained below).

⚠️ On the D, F and L, the stop switch is a "hydraulic switch" fitted in the brake line. Remove it and brake fluid leaks out; without bleeding the brakes, they won't work. For checks, remove only the two plugged-in wires. Don't loosen the switch body until you have fluid and bleeding gear ready.
⭐ The R alone is different: a "mechanical push switch" on the brake pedal mechanism (a different part, not in the fluid path, so the brakes still work if you remove it). The evidence from the originals is in the note at the very bottom.
⚠️ A car without brake lights gets hit from behind. Until it's fixed, avoid driving at night or in rain, and brake early to warn those behind. It is also a roadworthiness failure.
⚠️ Before touching the wiring, disconnect the battery's negative (−) terminal. This circuit dies with the key OFF, but the path up to it (fuse box, ignition switch) is always live.
How it works

A fuse after the key,and one red wire crossing from left to right

  1. The current comes from the battery to the fuse box and goes up from there to the ignition switch. Up to here it's the same path as in Parts 1 and 4.
  2. Turn the key and terminal 15/54 comes alive; from there it splits three ways: ① fuse F2 ② the coil (ignition) ③ the panel warning lights. — The split is before the fuse. So with F2 blown, the engine doesn't stop and the warning lights still come on.
  3. The yellow/black wire from F2 goes to the stop switch. When brake pressure (on the R, the push of the pedal) closes the contact, the red wire carries it down to the left rear of the car.
  4. The red wire reaches the left brake lamp first, and from there crosses under the floor to the right lamp. Left and right aren't fed separately.

Wire colours are the actual insulation colours on the car (AZZURRO = blue, GIALLO E NERO = yellow/black, ROSSO = red, NERO = black). Yellow dots = where current is flowing now. Faint dashed lines are "branches this story doesn't take"; other stories live there.

No brake lights with the key OFF is not a fault The brake lights take their power from fuse F2, after the ignition switch. So with the key out, pressing the brake doesn't light them. When someone helps you push-start, when you're towed, when you push the car on a slope — people behind you can't see you braking. This is how the 500 is designed, exactly as in the original wiring diagram (the blue wire from the ignition switch enters the supply side 15/54 of the fuse box).
Both "pressure switches", but working the opposite way to the oil warning light The oil pressure sender (engine oil) from Part 2 closes when there is no pressure, lighting the warning light, and opens as pressure rises. The stop switch (brake fluid), on the other hand, closes when pressure is applied, lighting the lamps. — Both are "contacts worked by pressure", but they work the opposite way, and the fluid itself is different. Mix them up and your tester readings come out exactly backwards. — Note that this contrast holds for the D, F and L (the R's stop switch is a push switch worked by your foot, not by pressure).
Narrowing it down

First see which one is out

Have someone stand behind the car, or press the brake facing a wall at night. Which side is out, left or right — that alone changes the list of suspects completely.

With a tester, measure the switch's two terminals with the brake pressed. 12 V on the near side and 0 V on the far side means the switch isn't closing (= the switch or the hydraulics). 0 V on both means fuse F2 or the ignition switch. 12 V on both means the switch is doing its job; suspect the red wire beyond it.

When something is wrong ①

Key OFF:this is not a fault

Brake pressed with the key out. The ignition switch contact is open, so everything after fuse F2 is dead. The brake contact is closed, but there's no power to begin with.

This scene comes first because many people chase the wiring here for nothing. By the way, the horn and interior light are alive even with the key OFF. In the same car, some things die with the key and some don't — that's the difference between fuses F1 and F2.

When something is wrong ②

Fuse F2 blown:takes the indicators and wiper with it

F2 blown. Key ON, brake pressed. Even so, no power reaches past the contact.

⭐ After F2 sit the brake lights, the indicators (flasher) and the wiper. So "no brake lights + no indicators" = almost certainly F2 alone.
On the other hand, the engine runs as if nothing happened, and the charge lamp and oil pressure warning light come on. The wires to the coil and the warning lights branch off before the fuse = at the ignition switch terminal. ⚠️ This is the exact mirror image of F1, the always-on supply (horn, interior light). The 500 has six fuses, and each one protects its own set of things, so which one blows determines clearly which combination dies (always-on F1, key-ON F2, separate F3/F4 for left and right headlights, F5/F6 for the side lights and number plate light).
⚠️ The indicators and wiper aren't drawn in this diagram. All we can say here is that "they sit after the same fuse"; trying it on the real car is the surest way.

When something is wrong ③

Only the right out,only the left out —which wire is broken

The wire crossing from the left lamp to the right is off. The left is lit, but only the right is out.

This time the left lamp's ground is off. Still "only one goes out", but the other side this time.

These two diagrams are the main point of this story. If even one lamp is lit, the whole path up to it is alive — the fuse, the switch, the red wire: the lit lamp itself proves it. So with a one-side symptom, you can narrow the suspects to two (bulb and ground) before getting the tester out.
⚠️ But the culprit for "only the right is out" can also be the crossover wire (first diagram above), so if the right bulb and ground are in the clear, go back to the left lamp and check where the crossover wire starts. The original wiring branches this wire off at the left lamp's socket.

When something is wrong ④

The other symptom:stuck on

The pedal is released, but the switch contact hasn't reopened (it's stuck). Both lamps stay lit.

Stuck on is harder to notice than not lighting, and nastier. Drivers behind can't tell whether you're slowing down, and it keeps draining the battery while parked (removing the key turns it off — that's the "same stuck switch, key removed" row in the cross-check above).
Besides the switch sticking inside, the cause can be a brake pedal that doesn't return (tired return spring, seized pivot) or pressure not fully releasing in the master cylinder. ⚠️ Those last two are dragging brakes in themselves, so don't dismiss them as a lamp problem. All this diagram can say is that "the contact stays closed"; why it stays closed lies outside the electrics.

Where it is on the car

This storyuses almost the full length of the car

● The ignition switch is in the middle of the dash, ● the fuse box at the front (in the trunk). The red wire from there runs along the left side to the very back, then crosses the floor to the right. It's one of the longest journeys in the 500's electrics.

⚠️ ● The two marks at the back show where the rear lamp lenses are. The brake lamps are inside them.

Once fixed

Press it andboth light up.

Key ON, press the brake. The left lamp lights, and the same current crosses the floor to light the right one — a 60-year-old design still doing its job in the same order. After the repair, always check too that they go out when you're not pressing.

⚠️There is no colour difference in the supply wire. In the F diagram the yellow/black wire looks plain yellow, but that is only because the black dashes of the two-colour wire aren't drawn; it is the same as in the D original: GIALLO E NERO. ⛔ Don't take "it's plain in the F diagram, so the colour differs" as evidence.
⚠️Which way the switch contact works is an inference. The original shows only a switch symbol and doesn't say whether the contact "closes under pressure" or "opens under pressure". Here it is read as "closes when brake pressure is applied" = the only way round in which a brake lamp works.
⭐The model difference in the stop switch lies outside this diagram. The D, F and L owner's handbooks describe this part as Interruttore, a pressione idraulica, per luci posteriori d'arresto (= a switch worked by hydraulic pressure). Meanwhile, the R handbook drops that "hydraulic", and the text states plainly azionato meccanicamente dal pedale freni, anziché idraulicamente (= worked mechanically by the brake pedal, not hydraulically). It is a different part too: the R's is the same push switch. ⭐The electrical path and the troubleshooting steps are the same for all four models; the only difference is whether the contact is closed by hydraulic pressure or by your foot.
