The starter won't turn

You pull the starter lever and get nothing at all. We follow the electrical path on a single diagram and narrow down how much of it is in the clear.

This story is about the FIAT 500 (D, F, L and R models). The wiring is drawn from the original 500 sources.

You've turned the key. Both warning lights are on (GENERAT. and OLIO = normal for a car at rest). And yet you pull the starter lever and the starter doesn't turn.

First make sure the gearbox is in neutral and the handbrake is on. On the 500, pulling the starter lever turns the starter even with the key out. If a gear is engaged, the car will move.
The starter draws hundreds of times the current of a warning light. If a tool on the battery's positive (+) terminal touches the body, enough current flows in that instant to burn the tool. Before touching terminals or heavy cables, disconnect the negative terminal first.
How it works

The loop that turns the starterNo key, no fuse in the path

  1. Pull the starter lever at the driver's seat and a cable moves the operating lever on top of the starter motor, which pushes the round gear (pinion) on the right of the diagram into the engine's flywheel. Once it's meshed, the contact closes — the meshing is done mechanically, not electrically.
  2. ⚠️ The electrical contact is on top of the starter motor, not at the driver's seat. All that's at the driver's seat is the starter lever, connected by a cable.
  3. ⚠️ If no cable comes to the starter motor from the driver's seat, the car has been fitted with a later starter with a solenoid. Cars that crank from the key or a button are like this. In that case the lever and cable story so far does not apply (the electrical input is different). First look at the starter on your own car and check whether a cable comes to it.
  4. When the contact closes, a short loop is made — battery positive terminal → heavy red cable → contact → heavy brown cable → starter → body ground — and it turns. There's no ignition switch and no fuse in this loop.

Wire colours are the actual insulation colours on the car (ROSSO = red, MARRONE = brown). ⚠️ Only the short black line under the starter is not a wire — the starter motor has no dedicated ground wire; the line stands for the path to ground through its mounting. Thick lines = cables that carry a lot of current in an instant. Yellow dots = where current is flowing now. Faded lines = where no power is reaching.

Don't misread it

Warning lights ondoesn't clear the starter

The branch to the warning lights leaves the starter loop at the topmost node. From there on, on the positive side, the warning lights and the starter never meet again (only the return path shares the engine and the ground strap = fault ②).

  1. Warning lights on = the battery up to the fork is alive. That's all it tells you.
  2. Below the fork, the starter loop is four things: the lever contact, the heavy brown cable, the motor, and the ground (its mounting). The warning lights go through none of these four = "lights on but no turning" is no contradiction; it means the suspects are narrowed to the lower half.

Conversely, warning lights on is no proof that "the battery is healthy". They need only a tiny current and will light even when there isn't enough left to turn the starter.

When something is wrong ①

Pulled, won't turn, warning lights still on

The upper half is alive and the lever contact is closed. Still it doesn't turn = the loop is broken somewhere in the lower half. The diagram above is an example where the starter ground (return path) is broken. Four candidates:

  1. The contact on top of the starter motor isn't closing (not enough lever travel, pitted contacts). The tell-tale sign is no sound when you pull. Go and look in the engine bay, not at your feet.
  2. The heavy brown cable, or the terminals at either end (contact side, starter side) off or loose.
  3. The starter itself (worn brushes, an internal break).
  4. A poor starter ground. ⚠️ The starter motor has no dedicated ground wire — the body of the starter is bolted to the engine, and from there the current returns through the flat braided strap between engine and body (the ground strap). Check for loose mounting bolts, rust or paint on the mating faces, and rust on the ground strap.

All this diagram can say is "broken or connected". On real cars the most common case is nothing broken, yet it won't turn = corroded terminals narrow the electrical path so it can't pass the heavy current the starter wants. In the diagram all of these look "connected". Narrowness is outside this diagram.

When something is wrong ②

If even the warning lights are off,it's the upper half

It's the same "won't turn", but if the warning lights are out too, the suspects change. The diagram above shows the heavy positive cable off the battery terminal = everything beyond it fades, and the starter and the warning lights die alike. The starter is in the clear.

  1. A flat battery (the most common).
  2. Loose positive or negative (−) terminals, white powder. Simply tightening them can fix it.
  3. The heavy positive cable or the battery ground cable itself has come off.
  4. A broken ground strap (the flat braided strap between engine and body). The engine sits on rubber mounts, isolated from the body, so if this breaks the starter and the warning light parts on the engine can all lose their return path together.

⭐ Even without a tester, there's one more thing you can do before ④ — a trick from the factory service manual: just pull the lever with the headlights on.
· The lights dim or go out = heavy current is flowing to the starter = the power is getting there. What's left is the starter itself or a weak battery.
· The lights don't change at all = no current is flowing = there's a break along the way. Go back to the heavy cables, the contact and the ground.
⭐ This is ④'s "measure the voltage drop", using the headlights instead of a voltmeter.

⚠️ After repeated "clunk but no turning", the gear may be jammed in the flywheel. In that case, put it in a low gear and rock the car back and forth to free it (this is the factory procedure too). ⛔ Once it's free, switch the key off first, then try starting again.

Where it is on the car

The places to touchare split between front and rear

● The battery is at the front of the car (in the trunk), ● the starter is right at the back. The heavy positive cable runs under the car from front to back. So there are terminals to tighten at both the front and the back. The ● in between is the starter lever at the driver's seat = mechanical; the electrical contact is at the back (on top of the starter motor) (the ● is shifted up a little to fit the drawing).

Once fixed

I tightened the ground.It turned.

The lever closes the contact; current from the battery runs down the heavy cable, through the motor windings and back to body ground. It turns only while the loop is closed. The warning lights were on all along, but they had nothing to do with this loop.

Once it fires, let go of the lever. If you keep pulling, the gear stays meshed with the spinning flywheel and the teeth chip. ⭐ Note that the pinion has a one-way drive (freewheel) = the moment the engine fires, the motor is not driven backwards from the engine side. What it protects is the inside of the motor, not the meshed teeth — so the reason to let go still stands (FIAT's own factory service standards and the Giardiniera service manual say the same).
