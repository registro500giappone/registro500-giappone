Side and tail lightsdoesn't light

The kind of fault you notice in a car park at night (the wiring diagram names them separately: side lights at the front, tail lights at the rear). This circuit has two fuses, and they are split not front/rear but crosswise, left to right. So the lights go out in a way that makes no sense when you look at the car: “front right and rear left go out together”. — And the green lamp in the speedo tells you which fuse it is.

This story is about the FIAT 500 (D, F, L and R). The wiring is taken from the 500's original sources.

Light switch on, but the rear stays dark

The light switch on the dash is on, yet the two rear lenses and the number plate light stay dark — that is the symptom on this page. Check with the key turned. This circuit is live with the key turned right and in the parking-light position, turned left (the reason is explained below).

⚠️ A car whose side and tail lights don't work becomes a car “nobody can see is there” at night and at dusk. In particular, a car with its rear tail lights out gets hit from behind. Until it's fixed, avoid driving at night, in the rain or through tunnels. A dead number plate light is also a roadworthiness defect.
⚠️ Before touching the wiring, disconnect the battery's negative (−) terminal. This circuit is live even with the key OFF = taking the key out does not make it safe.
⚠️ Don't replace a fuse with a “bigger” one. The two fuses cover five and four lights respectively (the high beams are also after these two). Raise the rating and the thin wiring they should protect burns first.
How it works

One light switch,front, rear and plate all light up

The column lever (low/high) doesn't appear in this circuit. Just switching on the light switch on the dash makes the front side lights, the rear tail lights, the number plate light and the green lamp in the speedo — the indicator that tells you they're on — all light up at once. The factory owner's handbook says so — “switching on lights the side lights and number plate light, and at the same time feeds the column lever circuit and the instrument lighting”.

Tap “Off” and “On” on the light switch in the diagram. Here too the key stays out.

Two fuses.Not front and rear, butcrosswise

The green wire from the light switch enters the fuse box as a single wire, and inside the box it splits into two fuses (F5, F6). So far, so simple. The odd part comes next —

  • F5 covers the front right side light and the rear left tail light — and the left high beam with its indicator (the blue lamp in the speedo that lights with high beam).
  • F6 covers the front left side light, the rear right tail light, the number plate light, the green lamp in the speedo — and the right high beam.

⚠️ On the R, the front side lights are inside the headlights (on the D, F and L they are separate parts). The fuses cover the same lights, but where you look on the car is different.

Front right and rear left are a pair. The wires crossing twice in the diagram above are not there to make the picture clearer. That is how the real car is wired, drawn as it is (in the original too, the front left and rear right lights get wires of the same colour).

⭐ And the high beams hang off the same two fuses. The column lever doesn't appear in this story, but it is part of what these fuses cover (F5 left, F6 right). It's a part the wiring diagram couldn't be traced through, but the factory owner's handbook has a table of “circuits each fuse protects”, and it is written there (→ note ② at the end). This will make the diagnosis one step clearer later on.

Light switch off =this is not a fault

The most common “fault that isn't a fault”. The light switch on the dash is simply off. In this state all six are out. The green lamp in the speedo is out too, so you can tell at once that “green off = no current is arriving at all”.

F5 blown =Front right and rear leftgo out together

Walk round the car and only front right and rear left are dark. Front left is on, rear right is on, the number plate light is on. — You might think two bulbs failed at once, but two diagonal bulbs almost never fail together. And the green lamp in the speedo stays on. That is what F5 looks like.

F6 blown =the green lamp goes out with them

This time four go out: front left, rear right, the number plate light and the green lamp in the speedo. From the driver's seat you can see the green isn't lit, so you have an idea before walking round. But “green off” alone doesn't prove it's F6 — with the light switch itself off (= the diagram just before the one above) the green goes out the same way. Step out and check whether front right and rear right are lit: if they are, it's F6; if everything is out, it's before the light switch.

If just one is out,it's not the fuse

The ground of the front left side light has come off. Only that one goes out; the other five stay on. A fuse takes out two or more at a time, so if “just one is out”, the fuse is innocent. Suspect three things: bulb, holder, ground. ⭐ The front side light and the indicator share one bulb with two filaments. So if that unit's indicator works but the side light doesn't, current reaches the bulb and the holder and ground are fine — only the side-light filament has broken.

Narrowing it down

From the green lamp in the speedo,work backwards

The strength of this circuit is that it has clues you can see from the driver's seat. The green lamp in the speedo — and, with high beam on, the blue indicator too. Go through them in order.

What you seeMost likely culprit

Fixed

All six are on. If you replace a fuse and the same one blows again, a wire somewhere in that group is touching the body. For F5 that's front right and rear left plus the left high beam; for F6 front left, rear right, the number plate light plus the right high beam — you only need to look at what that fuse covers, which is the advantage of having two.
