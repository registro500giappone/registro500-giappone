The interior light doesn't come on

You open the door, and the small bulb in the mirror stays dark. — This circuit has something none of the stories so far had: two ways back. Thanks to that, you can narrow down where it's broken without removing the bulb or the switch.

This story is about the FIAT 500 (D, F, L and R). The wiring is taken from the 500's original sources.

The door is open. The key position doesn't matter — the interior light should come on even with the key out. Yet the lens stays dark: that's the symptom on this page.

The interior light's purple wire is also live with the key out. It's after the same fuse F1 as the horn = a permanent supply. Before removing the mirror and touching the wiring, disconnect the battery's negative (−) terminal.
⚠️ Leave the built-in switch “On” when you get out, and it keeps draining the battery. On a car left for a few days, this is a quiet cause of “the starter won't turn in the morning” (though how many days it takes is a matter of current, which this diagram can't show).
⚠️ When replacing the bulb, keep the same wattage. A bigger one deforms the lens and the mirror's plastic with heat.
How it works

Two ways back —from the door,and from the mirror switch

  1. Current reaches fuse F1 by exactly the same route as Part 12 (horn). Leaving F1 it splits in two: one to the horn, the other along the purple wire to the bulb in the mirror.
  2. The bulb's positive (+) terminal always has 12V, regardless of the key. When it doesn't light, it's not that power isn't arriving but that the way back isn't closed.
  3. The wire from the bulb's negative terminal splits in two: ① the mirror's built-in switch (straight to ground) ② the door pillar switch (a black wire to the pillar, grounded there).
  4. If either one reaches ground, the bulb lights. That's why it lights when you open the door, even with the built-in switch “Off”.

Wire colours are the real insulation colours (VIOLA = purple, NERO = black). Yellow dots = where current is flowing now. The round tags on the wires are terminal names (+, 30, the bulb's + and −). The thin light lines inside the mirror box aren't wires from the original but the inside of the part (the original draws the interior light as a single part and only notes “there's a switch inside”).

“Open” the door and the switch goes “on” The door pillar switch is pushed in while the door is closed, and its contact is open. Open the door and the button pops out, closing the contact. — Pressed is “Off”, released is “On”. It's the opposite of what you'd expect, so if you mix this up when measuring with a meter, every conclusion is reversed. This diagram labels the switch by “the state of the door” (not by whether the contact is open or closed).

If you use a meter, measure the bulb's positive and negative terminals. 12V on both means power arrives but there's no way back; 0V on both means the fuse or the purple wire; 12V on the positive only and 0V on the negative means the bulb has blown (the voltage doesn't come through the filament).

When something is wrong ①

Fuse blown =it takes the horn down too

F1 blown. The door is open and return ② is connected, but no power is arriving in the first place. As this diagram shows, only two things are after the fuse: the horn and the interior light.

⭐ So “interior light off + horn silent too” is almost always explained by one fuse. Conversely, the charge lamp, the starter and the ignition are unaffected — the wire to the key branches off before the fuse (→ Part 4). This “only these two die together” combination is one of the clearest clues in the 500's electrics.

When something is wrong ②

Not with the door,but on with the switch

The door pillar switch's ground has come off. Even with the door open, return ② is a dead end and the bulb doesn't light.

Same fault, with the built-in switch “On”. It lights. — that alone confirms the bulb, the fuse and the purple wire are all alive.

These two diagrams are the main point of this story. Because there are two ways back, you can use one as a “test path”. Removing the mirror or getting out the meter can wait until after that. The pillar switch sits where rain and mud from your feet land, so a rusty ground is common (→ Part 5).

Where it is on the car

The purple wiresplits in two at the fuse box

The ● fuse box is at the front of the car. Of the two purple wires leaving it, one goes to the horn at the front and the other through the roof to the mirror. The door pillar switch is on the way, in the pillar at the base of the door.

⚠️ The interior light and the door pillar switch have no marks. The marks are the two for the ● battery and the ● fuse box.

Once fixed

Pillar ground cleaned.Open the doorand it lights.

The built-in switch stays “Off”. Yet just opening the door lights it — that's the normal state. It's only a feature that saves you reaching up when unloading at night, but these two ways back show that a car from 60 years ago was wired with that much thought.

All lighting and colours in the diagrams are calculated results; no numbers were made to fit the pictures. ⭐ The interior light and the horn are the only two loads on the load side of fuse F1 (in the original F diagram the load side of the fuse box has two VIOLA wires = horn and interior light; the interior light row in the D original, p40, also reads “permanent supply + built-in SW → ground”, with one more NERO going out to the door pillar side).
⚠️Purple (VIOLA) is the F, L and R colour. Only on the D is the same wire white (BIANCO). Only the colour differs; the connections and the number of wires are the same (F, L and D checked on the factory wiring diagrams; the R's factory diagram is printed too coarsely to read the colour names, so it was checked on a wiring diagram distributed by a parts dealer).
⚠️The real car has two door pillar switches (left and right doors), wired in parallel. This diagram uses one to stand for both = the result, “grounded if either is open”, is the same. If only one door doesn't work, read this diagram's single switch as two.
⚠️The built-in switch positions are inferred. The original only says “built-in SW → ground” and doesn't show how many positions the contact has. Here we took it as two positions, “Off/On” (with this reading the door lights it even with the switch “Off” = matching the real car). ⛔ We have not read it as three positions “Off / On / door” — three positions would let you switch the door function off, which changes the behaviour.
⚠️What this diagram doesn't have:
①Blown bulb = this diagram only checks “whether wires are connected”, so it can't make a scene with a broken bulb filament (covered by ④ in the table above and by how to read the meter).
②Lights "sometimes" from a bad contact = it has no timeline for on-and-off. If wiggling the mirror makes it flicker, it's usually the built-in switch contact or the lens contacts.
③Parasitic drain = how many days until the battery goes flat with the switch left on. That's about the amount of current (a matter for measurement).
