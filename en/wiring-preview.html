How to read the terminals

A red circle marks a terminal — the point where a wire is connected to a component. The white tag is that terminal’s designation, transcribed exactly as printed in the wiring diagram of the FIAT owner’s handbook. The numbers are DIN terminal designations (the German standard), and you will often find the same number stamped on the part itself, for example on the regulator.

Power and earth

30
Permanent supply from the battery. Live even with the key out (30/2, 30/3, 30/4 are branches of that same permanent supply).
15/54
Supply that is live only with the ignition switched on.
31
Earth (the body, negative side).

Charging and starting

51
Dynamo output (positive side).
67
Field terminal on the regulator.
+
The component’s own positive terminal (battery, ignition coil, starter, flasher unit).

Lighting

56a
Main beam.
56/a1, 56/a2
Main beam split left and right (56/a1 = left, 56/a2 = right).
56b
Dipped beam.
56/b1, 56/b2
Dipped beam split left and right at the fuses (56/b1 = left, 56/b2 = right).
57
Position (side) light circuit. On the instrument it feeds the tell-tale for the position lights.

Letter markings

Tags that are not numbers are the markings given to the internal contacts of that component: C, F, INT on the wiper motor, C, D, H, L on the wiper switch, +, P, L on the flasher unit, 2, L, 5 on the turn signal switch, C, INT on the exterior light selector switch (the column commutator), L on the L-model dashboard light switch and E, F on its exterior light switch, and INTER (the ignition-on supply) or 0 (oil pressure warning — olio) on the instrument.

One and the same wire is called something different at each end. The light blue wire in the wiper circuit, for instance, is “F” at the motor and “C” at the switch. A designation is not the name of the wire but the name of the terminal on the component.

Which models show terminals

Terminals are available for all four models — D, F, L and R (the D uses the same data as the F, since all 16 circuits are wired identically). On the L we show only the markings we could confirm in the original handbook: parts whose markings are not printed there at all, such as the fuse box and the flasher unit, are left without labels. On the R we show only the terminals it shares with the F: the instrument panel is left out, because the R has a fuel gauge and a different set of terminals there.

For the fuse box contacts only, the designations are taken from the factory D diagram: the factory F (and D) diagram marks the fuses by position (1–6, A–F) alone, and the D is confirmed to be wired identically. That transfer cannot be used on the L, whose fuse assignment differs from the F, so no tags are shown on the L fuse box. Terminals that carry no designation in the original diagram (rear lamps, horn, door switches, fuel sender and so on) are left untagged. Details vary with year, market and modifications — always check against the actual car before working on it.

How the D, F, L and R differ

The F, L and R are each the actual diagram of that model; the D is a reference drawing this site produced from the F diagram. Nothing has to be read across. Pick the one you want under “Model” above.

Common to D / F / L / R

  • System: 12V, negative earth
  • Battery: 32Ah
  • Charging: dynamo, 16A continuous (not an alternator)
  • Starter: 0.5kW
  • Wire colours — marked on the loom in Italian: Azzurro=light blue / Giallo=yellow / Grigio=grey / Nero=black / Rosso=red / Rosa=pink / Verde=green / Viola=purple / Bianco=white / Marrone=brown

F and D

Compared against the factory D wiring diagram, all 16 circuits are identical in connections and wire colours. The D version is therefore the same drawing as the F with only the model letter in the title changed. The one difference is in how the routing around the panel light switch (legend 9) is drawn. This diagram follows the F's routing, whereas on the factory D diagram the switch sits on the supply side (ignition-on supply → switch → lamp). Either way, how it works and is operated is the same.

F and R

As drawings, only three circuits differ: wiper, turn signals and instrument panel. In the other 13 circuits not a single wire is different.

F and L

The differences are almost entirely inside the cabin (dashboard through to the instruments). The engine bay is identical to the F: charging and ignition are the same drawing on every model.

What the L adds:

  • Fuel gauge (sender in the tank + gauge in the instrument) … the F has no fuel gauge and runs on the reserve tap. This is where nearly all of the extra wiring is.
  • Warning lights brought together in one instrument panel (Quadro di controllo): fuel reserve, charging, oil pressure, high beam, position lights and turn signals, plus panel illumination.
    Note: the F has these warning lights too — the L simply groups them into a single panel.
