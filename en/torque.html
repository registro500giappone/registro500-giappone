🔩 Tightening Torque Reference

⚠️ Before you use this (please read)
  • The figures here are for reference. The final decision is yours, together with your own workshop manual.
  • Brakes, hubs, wheels, steering, seat belts and anything else marked ⚠️ can cause an accident on the road if you get them wrong.
  • Even the same fixing can take a different figure depending on the year, the specification and the parts fitted.
  • If a thread is rusted or galling, reaching the specified torque will not produce the clamping force it should.
  • About the entries that say the workshop manuals give no figure. For each of them the tightening-torque tables and the running text of every source used here (the English workshop manual, the German factory manual with its Giardiniera supplement, and the 126 manuals) have been checked and no figure for that point was found. Since looking further will not produce one, use the general guide by thread size at the foot of the page. The manufacturer not specifying a figure does not mean the fixing can be left loose.
  • Some points are given different figures by different sources. Where that happens the entry is marked "⚠ Sources disagree" and both figures are shown with the reason. Which one to follow is your decision.
  • Every entry has been compiled independently by cross-checking several workshop manuals; none of them is a reproduction of any one manual. The source is stated on each entry. This site is not connected with Fiat or with any of the publishers.

🔧 Guide by thread size

A general guide for fixings that are not in this reference. These are not the specified figures for any particular model. They assume steel into steel, a coarse thread and dry (unlubricated) fastening.
