The oil pressure warning light won't go out

Also called the oil light or oil pressure lamp (on the car it is the small window "OLIO" at the lower right of the speedometer). We follow the electrical path on a single diagram and narrow down what is wrong.

This story is about the FIAT 500 (D, F, L and R models). The wiring is drawn from the original 500 sources.

The engine is running (you're driving), yet the small window OLIO at the lower right stays lit red. That is the symptom on this page.

Stop the engine first. Look for the cause after that. This warning light is the signal that "oil is not being pushed through the engine". If you keep running it with genuinely no oil pressure, the crankshaft bearings are damaged in a short time (the kind of damage that means taking the engine out). Stop somewhere safe, switch off, and first look at the dipstick. If the oil is low, top it up.
But — this lamp lights in exactly the same way for an electrical fault. If the oil is at the right level and the lamp stays on, split the problem with the diagrams below.

You can start splitting the problem by disconnecting just one wire. Before that, one minute on why this lamp lights at all — once you see the logic, you can see where the electrics end and the oil begins.

How it works

Why it lights with the key ONand goes out once the engine starts

The oil pressure warning light is also a small bulb placed along an electrical path. Close the loop and it lights; break it and it goes out — and what opens and closes that loop is the oil pressure sender.

  1. While stopped, the pressure is zero. The contact of the sender screwed into the engine is closed, taking the lower side of the lamp to body ground.
  2. When the engine runs the oil pump builds pressure, and that pressure pushes the contact open.
  3. The lower side of the lamp becomes a dead end = no current = out. In other words, "it went out" is the signal that there is oil pressure.

Wire colours are the actual insulation colours on the car (ROSSO = red, AZZURRO = light blue, GRIGIO = grey, MARRONE = brown). Yellow dots = where current is flowing now. Faded lines = where no power is reaching.

A common question With the engine running, put a tester on the grey wire (GRIGIO) and it shows 12 V. That isn't a fault; it is normal — 12 V arrives from the upper side of the lamp, and the lower side is a dead end, so the voltage gets there but no current flows. That is why, in the diagram above, the grey wire stays coloured while the warning light is out; the absence of yellow dots is what says "nothing is flowing". This is what lies behind "the tester reads, but it's still not fixed".
Don't misread it

This warning lightonly watches "oil pressure"

As the diagram shows, this circuit has just one pressure switch. So this warning light can tell you only one thing — whether the oil pressure has dropped below the lower limit. Put the other way round:

  1. It doesn't watch the oil level. Even if the oil is low, the lamp stays out as long as there is pressure (and the other way round: with the right amount but no pressure, it lights).
  2. It doesn't watch how dirty or hot the oil is. There is no other switch and no other wire.
  3. It doesn't know how high the pressure is either. Only two states: lit or not. "Barely enough" and "plenty to spare" are the same "out".

So "it's out" doesn't mean "all is well" — only that the pressure is above the lower limit. Don't let this warning light stand in for checking the oil level.

⭐And there is one more thing the factory owner's handbook states clearly — when the engine is fully warm, at low revs such as idling, this warning light may come on even though nothing is wrong. Warm oil is thinner, and at low revs the pump delivers less, so the pressure drops to just about the lower limit. If it goes out when you raise the revs, that is normal. ⚠️ If it stays on while you are driving, that is another matter — stop right away.

The factory service manual gives that "lower limit" as 0.5–1.5 kg/cm² (the contact switches somewhere in this range). Not an oil pressure gauge, but a switch that reports the lower limit once — that is what the points above come down to.

When something is wrong

Lit with the engine running =Suspect: the lower half

Lit while running means the lower side of the lamp is still grounded. The upper half, up to the key and the speedometer, is in the clear; only the part from the lamp down is suspect. But — electrics alone can't tell these three apart:

  1. The oil pressure really is low (low oil, oil pump, worn bearings). = The sender is doing its job and the contact staying closed is correct. Suspect this first.
  2. A faulty oil pressure sender (the contact no longer opens even under pressure).
  3. The grey wire (GRIGIO) is touching the body (insulation chafed through, shorting to ground).

Each of these leads to exactly the same state: "the lower side of the lamp is grounded". Whichever of the three it is, you get this same single diagram — which is exactly why the next step disconnects one wire to split them.

It's tempting to suspect a fuse, but this lamp's path doesn't go through a fuse (terminal 30 of the ignition switch branches off the supply side of fuse F1). For the same reason as with the charge lamp, checking fuses won't fix this symptom.

Narrowing it down

Disconnect one wire, andyou can tell electrics from oil

Do this with the engine stopped and only the key ON (there's no need to start the engine). If the grey wire is touching the body, the symptom shows even with the engine stopped.

Where it is on the car

The "oil pressure sender"Here on your car

The grey wire comes off a small switch screwed into the engine block, at the rear, in the engine bay (●). The dynamo (●) is there only as a landmark to get your bearings. The lamp is in the speedometer at the driver's seat, so this story spans the front and back of the car.

Once fixed

I topped up the oil.It went out.

It didn't just go out — you now know why it goes out: oil pressure pushed the contact open and the lower side of the lamp became a dead end. The grey wire stays coloured (12 V still reaches it) and only the yellow dots have gone. That is what "normal" looks like for this circuit.

A side question: "The engine sits on rubber mounts, isolated from the body — is the sender's ground all right?" — The sender's ground runs engine block → engine ground strap → body. The original wiring diagram draws no wire here either, just a ground symbol (meaning the threaded body is itself the ground). The current through the warning light is tiny, so a slightly weak ground here won't change how it lights — it shows up first as "the starter won't turn".
