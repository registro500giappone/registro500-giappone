Indicatorsoff

You move the indicator lever and nothing lights. Or the front lights but the rear doesn't. The 500's indicators light as a set of three: front, side and rear — the side one is the small lamp on the side of the front wing. If you know where these three split, the combination of lamps that are out alone tells you where the break is.

This story is about the FIAT 500 (D, F, L and R). The wiring is taken from the 500's original sources.

The indicator lever is moved, yet nothing lights — that is the symptom on this page. Turn the key. The 500's indicators don't work unless the key is turned (that's where they differ from the headlights and side lights).

⚠️ A car whose indicators don't work is a car that can't tell anyone where it's turning. Until it's fixed, give hand signals every time you turn or change lanes (in Japan: arm straight out of the window for right, arm bent upward for left). One side not working is the more dangerous case — because you drive believing it works.
⚠️ Before touching the wiring, disconnect the battery's negative (−) terminal. This circuit dies with the key OFF, but right next to it in the same fuse box there are wires that stay live with the key off (horn and interior light).
⚠️ Don't replace a fuse with a “bigger” one. The fuse protecting this circuit also covers the brake lights and the wiper. Raise the rating and the thin wiring it should protect burns first.
How it works

Left and right splitonly inside the indicator lever

Turn the key and current goes through fuse F2 to the flasher — the little can that makes the on-and-off. From there it passes to the indicator lever, and only inside it does it split into left and right. ⭐ From the flasher to the indicator lever it's one wire shared by both sides. So if this comes off, neither left nor right works.

⚠️ This indicator lever is a second lever, separate from the headlight lever (Part 8). According to the original owner's handbook, up is right, down is left, and it returns to neutral by itself when the steering wheel straightens.

Tap the indicator lever in the diagram: up, neutral, down. The key stays ON.

⚠️ In this diagram the flasher is drawn as connecting when the indicator lever is moved. The real one doesn't work like that. The flasher is a part that switches itself on and off by its own heat when current flows; the lever doesn't move its contacts. This diagram has no flashing at all, so look only at “on or off”. Symptoms such as flashing too fast or too slow are covered by the diagnosis table below.

Front and side: one wire,the rear: another

What happens after the indicator lever is the heart of this part. The three on the left are fed by two separate wires.

  • Front and side = one wire goes from the indicator lever to the front lamp, and at the front lamp it passes on to the side lamp. So when the front goes out, the side goes out with it.
  • Rear = a separate wire runs from the indicator lever to the back of the car. Even if front and side go out, the rear stays lit.

Put the other way, if “the front is out but the side is lit”, the problem is after the split — that is, the front lamp itself (bulb, holder, ground). The combination of lamps that are out narrows the search at a stroke.

⚠️ In the diagram, the wires to the rear are routed outside the row of lamps — so the wires don't appear to cross; it doesn't follow the real car's routing. Where a wire branches there is a black dot.

The green lamp in the panel:there is only one

The green indicator lamp in the panel is fed by a single wire from the flasher's output. Left or right, the same single lamp lights. ⭐ So the indicator lamp can't tell you which side is on. — But it is a strong tool for diagnosis: if the green is lit, everything up to the flasher is alive.

Indicator lever in neutral =this is not a fault

The indicator lever is in neutral. Current reaches the flasher but goes no further. All six being off is normal. ⚠️ On the real car the break is inside the indicator lever, but in this diagram it is drawn as a break at the flasher — because of the approximation explained above. — Also, with the key OFF, nothing lights in the same way. This circuit is after fuse F2, and that isn't a fault either.

F2 blown =the brake lights die too

All seven go out. ⭐ This is the same fuse as the brake lights in Part 7. So if “the indicators and the brake lights died together”, fuse F2 alone explains it — it's not several bulbs failing at once. The wiper is on the same fuse too. The horn, on the other hand, sounds (it's after a different fuse, F1).

The flasher doesn't work

The flasher itself has stopped working. Neither side lights, and neither does the green lamp in the panel. It looks exactly like a blown F2, so check the fuse first, then suspect this part. ⭐ Whether the brake lights work tells them apart — if the brake lights work but no indicator does, the fuse is fine and the suspects narrow to the flasher and the wire before it.

Front and side out,only the rear stays

The wire from the indicator lever to the front left has come off. Front left and side left are out; only rear left is lit. The side lamp branches off at the front lamp, so when the front dies the side goes with it. — Only the rear left on: that is what this fault looks like.

If just one is out,it's about that lamp

The ground of the front left lamp has come off. Only that one goes out; side and rear stay on. ⭐ At the front, the side light and the indicator share one bulb (one bulb with two filaments. At the rear, side light, brake light and indicator sit in one housing). So if “the side light works but the indicator doesn't”, current reaches that bulb and the holder and ground are fine — only the indicator filament has broken.

Narrowing it down

Read the combinationof lamps that are out

Walk round the car and count which ones are out. The combination alone narrows the suspects this far.

⚠️This diagram can't show the flashing speed. This diagram only checks whether wires are connected and it has no passage of time. ⭐ And —“A failed bulb makes it flash faster” is not always true. Which way it goes depends on the flasher fitted.
· The original thermal type = a part that heats a metal strip with the current to switch on and off. When one bulb fails, the current drops, it heats more slowly, and it flashes slower, stays on, or the indicator lamp stops working — the factory workshop manual also says “when a bulb fails, the indicator lamp doesn't light”.
· Electronic replacements = sense the drop in current and make it flash faster (the same as modern cars). ⭐Most replacement parts on sale today are this type, and many cars actually have one, so flashing faster doesn't mean something is wrong.
Either way, the change in speed is itself the sign that “one bulb on that side has failed”. Check the three on that side first.

Fixed

The three on the left and the green lamp in the panel are on. If you replace the fuse and it blows again, a wire somewhere in what F2 covers — indicators, brake lights, wiper — is touching the body. If it blows only when you use the indicators, the search is limited to this diagram.
