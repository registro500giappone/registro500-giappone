The wipersStopped

It starts to rain, you switch on, and the wipers don't move. Or you switch off and they won't stop. The 500's wipers have two wires between the switch and the motor: one to drive them, the other a “stopping wire”. Look at these two separately and the suspects for “won't move” and “won't stop” split cleanly.

This story is about the FIAT 500 (D, F, L and R). The wiring is taken from the 500's original sources.

You switch on and neither wiper moves — that is the symptom on this page. Turn the key. The 500's wipers don't work unless the key is turned (that's where they differ from the headlights and side lights). ⭐ Both wipers are driven by one motor. So if “only one doesn't move”, it isn't electrical; it's the linkage (the bars joining the arms).

⚠️ Don't drive in the rain if your wipers don't work. The air-cooled 500's windscreen is small and mists up easily, and if it can't be wiped you really can't see ahead. If it starts raining, stop and wait — that's the safest thing.
⚠️ Before touching the wiring, disconnect the battery's negative (−) terminal. This circuit dies with the key OFF, but right next to it in the same fuse box there are wires that stay live with the key off (horn and interior light).
⚠️ Don't replace a fuse with a “bigger” one. The fuse protecting this circuit also covers the brake lights and the indicators. Raise the rating and the thin wiring it should protect burns first.
How it works

Even switched off,the motor still has power

Turn the key and current reaches the far side of fuse F2. From there one wire goes to the wiper switch — so far the same shape as the other stories. ⭐ But the 500 also connects another wire straight to the motor, “without passing the switch”. So even with the switch off, as long as the key is on, the motor's terminal stays live.

This isn't a wiring mistake. It's the wire that, when you switch the wipers off, brings the arms back down to the bottom of the windscreen before stopping, instead of leaving them standing. It needs power to keep moving after switching off, so it's taken from outside the switch.

Tap the switch in the diagram, On and Off. The key stays ON. When you switch off, watch that the wire running down the far left (blue/black) keeps its colour.

⚠️ This diagram doesn't have the return-to-park movement after switching off. That movement is made by a contact inside the motor that opens and closes in step with the shaft's rotation, but the original wiring diagram doesn't draw the inside of the motor (it's just a box with four terminals). So in this diagram those two wires only show that they are “connected” — no yellow dots run on them either. ⭐ But we do know what's inside — we open it next.

The wire that drives it:only one of the two

Keep the two wires running straight down from the switch box apart in your mind. ⚠️ The blue/black running down the far left of the diagram is a different wire (the one that skips the switch — seen above).

  • The right one straight down (blue) = the drive wire. When you switch on, current goes through it to the motor. ⭐ When the wipers won't move, this is the one to suspect.
  • The left one straight down (blue/white) = the stopping wire. It's used after switching off, to bring the arms back to the park position. ⭐ While switched On, the wipers work even if this wire is off — which gives the symptom “it works, but stops in an odd place when switched off”.

⚠️ In this diagram the blue/white wire always stays faded. It only works while the contact inside the motor is closed, and this diagram doesn't have that contact (which is why we open the motor in the next section). Faded does not mean broken.

⚠️ Only one wire colour differs by model. This “stopping wire” is BIANCO (white) on the D and AZZURRO-BIANCO (blue/white) on the F, L and R. ⭐ The F, L and R also have exactly the same connections (what goes where). ⚠️ Only the D's switch is connected differently — explained below in “Only the D parks by hand”.

Inside the motor,what is happening?

From here on it's what's shown not in the wiring diagram but in the factory workshop manual. ⭐ The wiring diagram tells you “what connects to what”; the workshop manual tells you “how it works” — another book about the same car.

Inside the wiper motor there are three windings and one contact that opens and closes as the shaft turns.

Inside the motor (schematic). The four at the top are the four we've been following. ⭐ The far-left wire (blue/black) comes in at C and passes through this contact D — which is why “power arrives even when switched off” matters.

  • A = series winding. Fed from INT, it turns the armature. ⭐ When switched “On”, this is what drives the wipers.
  • B = shunt winding. It sits in parallel with the armature and steadies the rotation.
  • S = auxiliary winding. ⭐ Few turns of thick wire — the workshop manual goes out of its way to say so. It's a winding for turning slowly and strongly, and it works only when parking.
  • D = cam contact. It passes the current from C only until the arms reach the park position.

The camstops it

What opens and closes this contact D is a fan-shaped piece fixed to the reduction gear's pinion. ⭐ The workshop manual itself notes that “in the circuit diagram it is simplified and drawn as a cam” — the real thing is less a cam than a lug on a gear.

Left = while turning. The segment is clear of the contact, and the contact stays closed. Right = the moment the arms reach the park position. The segment pushes the contact up and opens it, the current is cut, and it stops.

Put together, it goes like this. Switch off → the connections inside the switch change over and the motor's F terminal goes to ground → auxiliary winding S starts working → the motor turns slowly and strongly, kept going by the current from the C terminal → at the park position the segment opens contact D → it stops. ⭐ That is what's behind “switch off, and they return to the bottom of the windscreen before stopping”.

Only the Dparks by hand

⚠️ So far this is about the F, L and R. ⭐ The D's wiper switch has three positions, up and down.

  • Up = Run. The wipers move.
  • Middle = Off. ⚠️ It stops on the spot — even with the arms standing up, they stop right there.
  • Down = Park. ⭐ Only while you hold it here, the auxiliary winding S circuit we just saw is connected and the arms return to the park position. Once they're back, let go (a spring returns it to “Off” automatically).

On the wiring diagram this difference shows up as the number of wires into the switch. ⭐ The D's switch has a wire to the motor's C terminal — which is why it can send power “only while held down”. The F, L and R switch has no such terminal; the C terminal is fed straight from the fuse — so in exchange for parking by itself when switched off, it always has power while the key is on.

⚠️ This diagram shows the F, L and R wiring. ⭐ If you drive a D, read only the part “the motor has power even when switched off” differently — on the D, power comes only while you hold the lever down. Everything else (what to suspect when it won't move, the same fuse F2, the ground, the inside of the motor) is the same on all four models.

Fuse F2:the third one

Fuse F2, which protects the wipers, is the same fuse as the brake lights (Part 7) and the indicators (Part 10). ⭐ If all three die together, it's not three things failing at once — one fuse explains it.

On the other hand, the horn sounds — it's after a different fuse (F1). The engine starts too, because the ignition wire branches off before this fuse (Part 6). ⭐ “The engine starts, but wipers, brake lights and indicators are all dead” — that is what F2 looks like.

Key OFF:this is not a fault

The switch is on, but the key is OFF. Not moving is normal. The 500's wipers are after fuse F2 and don't work unless the key is turned. ⭐ Turn the key and try again — that's the first wrong turn to rule out.

F2 blown

When the fuse blows, both wires to the motor die at once. ⭐ At the same time the brake lights and indicators are dead too. Put the other way, if someone presses the brake and the lights come on, fuse F2 is fine — you can check without getting out, before looking at the fuse.

Between switch and motor,one wire comes off

The switch is on and the fuse is good, yet nothing moves. The drive wire (blue) has come off. ⭐ At this point the far-left wire (blue/black) still has its colour — because power reaching the motor's terminal and the motor turning are two different things.

Ground off =power arrives, but it won't turn

The motor's ground (its way back) has come off. ⭐ Power does reach the motor's terminals. It still won't turn — current does no work unless it can go and come back. The wiper motor is often just bolted to the body, and rust or paint on those bolts becomes a bad ground.

The other symptom =switched off, but it won't stop

The switch is set to Off, but its contacts haven't returned. The motor keeps running. ⭐ Take the key out and it stops — that's the row “still stuck, key taken out” in the verification at the very bottom of this page.

⚠️ “Won't stop” has another cause this diagram can't show. It's cam contact D, which we saw earlier. If this contact gets dirty and stops conducting, the arms stop part-way instead of returning to the park position; if instead the segment can no longer open the contact, they keep turning slowly — ⭐ the workshop manual lists exactly these two as faults. This diagram can only tell whether the switch contacts stay closed. ⛔ Just watching it keep running won't tell you whether it's the switch or the inside of the motor — both look the same: “won't stop”.
⭐ To tell them apart, check the switch first. The switch is a part inside this diagram. Remove it and see whether its terminals stay connected when set to “Off”, and that settles it. If the switch is fine, the suspicion moves outside this diagram — into the motor.

Narrowing it down

Won't it run,or won't it stop?

What you seeMost likely culprit
Doesn't move at all, and no brake lights or indicatorsFuse F2 (all three are dead together)
Doesn't move at all, brake lights workThe fuse is fine. The switch / the switch-to-motor wire / the motor ground
Won't move, but starts when you tap the motorWorn brushes in the motor (power is arriving)
Won't stop when switched offThe switch contacts haven't returned, or the contact inside the motor. ⭐ They look the same, so first remove the switch and check continuity
When switched off it stops part-way, doesn't return to parkThe contact inside the motor / the stopping wire (blue/white) is off
Only one arm doesn't moveNot electrical: the linkage (the bars joining the arms). Both are driven by one motor
Works but very slowly, stops when the rain makes it heavyGround resistance / brushes / seized linkage (wet the glass and move it gently by hand)
The fuse blows againA wire somewhere in what F2 covers — wiper, brake lights, indicators — is touching the body

⚠️ “Slow” and “heavy” can't be shown in this diagram. It only checks whether wires are connected and has no notion of electrical strength (voltage drop). ⭐ If the wipers are slow, first take the arms off and run the motor alone — if it then turns freely, the cause isn't electrical but a seized linkage.

⚠️⭐ If you take the arms off, how tightly you refit them matters. The factory workshop manual says plainly that “if the arm's retaining nut works loose, the motor burns out”. A loose arm slips and stalls part-way while the motor keeps straining — a wiping arc that's gradually getting narrower may be the start of it. Tighten it when you notice. That alone saves a motor.

Fixed

Switched on, the yellow dots go all the way round: fuse → switch → motor → body ground. ⭐ Look once more: the far-left wire (blue/black) has colour too. When you switch off, the motor takes power from this wire to return to the park position.
