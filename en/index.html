Two tools for classic FIAT 500 & 126 owners

Registro500 Giappone is a register of air-cooled FIAT 500 and 126 owners in Japan. The site is written in Japanese, but two of the tools on it are just as useful anywhere in the world — so we have put them into English. Both are free and need no account.

Tightening Torque Reference

110 tightening torques for the 500 and 126, searchable by the name of the fixing. N·m, kgf·m and lb·ft are shown side by side, with a guide by thread size for the fixings the factory never specified.

Wiring Diagram Viewer

The FIAT 500 wiring for the D, F, L and R, each split into 16 circuits. Pick one — headlights, turn signals, wiper — and only that circuit lights up while the rest go grey. Terminal designations can be overlaid on the diagram.

How these were made

The torque figures are compiled from five workshop manuals: the Autobooks manual for the 500, Fiat's own German-language manual with its Giardiniera supplement, and Haynes for both the 500 and the 126. Of the 110 entries, 80 are confirmed by two or more of those manuals and 54 by three or more.

Where the manuals disagree, both figures are shown with their sources rather than one being chosen for you. Nothing is converted from one unit to another to fill a gap, and nothing is invented — 11 entries simply record that the factory never specified a figure. Every entry carries its source.

The wiring diagram was drawn by OTSUKA from an actual F and separated into circuit layers by Sushio. It appears here in English and Italian with the artist's permission. The L and R are the actual diagrams for those models; the D is a reference drawing our club produced from the F after confirming that all 16 circuits are wired identically.

⚠️ Before you use them

These are reference figures, not a replacement for your own manual and your own judgement. Anything marked ⚠️ — brakes, hubs, wheels, steering, seat belts — can cause an accident on the road if it is wrong. Figures also vary with year, market and the parts actually fitted, and a seized or corroded fastener will not reach its clamping force however carefully you torque it.

This site is independent and is not connected with Fiat or with any of the publishers named above.
